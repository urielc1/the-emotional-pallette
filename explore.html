02 / YOUR VOICE

Our interpretations.

01 / VINCENT VAN GOGH

The Starry Night

[OBSERVATION] I notice the thick, swirling bands of cobalt and ultramarine blue sweeping across the night sky, punctuated by glowing yellow haloes around the stars and crescent moon, while a dark cypress tree rises sharply in the foreground.

[EMOTION] This makes me feel a mix of restless awe and quiet wonder because the turbulent brushstrokes make the night feel alive and constantly in motion rather than still or empty.

[INTERPRETATION] Another viewer might feel uneasy or overwhelmed instead because the relentless movement of the sky and the shadowy, towering cypress can read as emotional turbulence rather than peaceful stargazing.

02 / CLAUDE MONET

Bridge over a Pond of Water Lilies

[OBSERVATION] I notice the arched green Japanese footbridge spanning across a dense tapestry of layered greens, soft pinks, and dappled reflections of willow branches on the pond's surface.

[EMOTION] This makes me feel grounded and calm because the lush, overlapping shades of green mirror the quiet relief of stepping into a shaded garden on a warm afternoon.

[INTERPRETATION] This setting could also suggest nostalgia or gentle solitude to another visitor because the blurred, painterly edges make the scene feel like a fading memory rather than a crisp physical place.

03 / KATSUSHIKA HOKUSAI

The Great Wave off Kanagawa

[OBSERVATION] I notice the towering crest of Prussian blue water curling into sharp, foaming waves above three narrow wooden boats, while a small, snowy Mount Fuji sits completely still in the background.

[EMOTION] This makes me feel tense yet strangely steady because the sheer scale of the curling wave creates immediate suspense, even as the distant mountain anchors the horizon.

[INTERPRETATION] The contrast between the chaotic, surging blue wave and the unmoving silhouette of Mount Fuji might suggest the resilience of nature, or remind viewers how fragile human effort is when measured against the ocean.
